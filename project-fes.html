Foundation for Educational Services

A solo UI/UX design contribution created over approximately two months for an employer tender proposal in 2025. The concept explores how a mobile-first interface system could bring several family-facing services into one clear, consistent experience.

Proposal Context

Designing a credible service concept for a tender

This work was produced as part of Grant Thornton Malta's tender proposal for the Foundation for Educational Services. I was responsible for the UI/UX design, translating the proposal requirements into a structured mobile-first interface concept.

The tender was not awarded, so the interface was not implemented or tested with end users. The screens are presented as a proposal-stage design study rather than a launched product.

The Challenge

The proposal needed to account for payments, documents, bookings, child information, and pickup verification. The design challenge was to give these different tasks a shared interaction model, particularly on mobile, while retaining the clarity expected from important family-facing actions.

01

Simplify Complex Tasks

Make frequently used services easy to understand and complete without overwhelming the user.

02

Create Consistency

Give payments, documents, bookings and child-related services a shared interaction language.

03

Design To Scale

Build reusable interface patterns that allow additional services and content to fit naturally into the platform.

Design Approach

Within the proposal scope, I began with the service structure and recurring tasks, then developed repeatable interface patterns that could make different journeys feel related. Without direct user research, these decisions were treated as hypotheses to be validated if the proposal progressed.

  1. 01

    Review

    Review the tender requirements and identify the services the concept needed to represent.

  2. 02

    Structure

    Group related tasks and establish a proposed mobile-first information hierarchy.

  3. 03

    Explore

    Compare navigation and layout approaches for recurring service journeys.

  4. 04

    Design

    Create reusable patterns for cards, forms, actions, status, and feedback.

  5. 05

    Refine

    Refine visual consistency, readability, and accessibility considerations across the proposal.

Proposed User Flow

Making child pickup clear from selection to confirmation

Child pickup demonstrates the intended interaction model: move from a clear dashboard entry point to pickup management, authorised-person review, and verification setup without losing the user's place.

Clear Next Action

The proposed hierarchy gives prominence to actions such as Show Card, Quick Auth, and Add New Person.

Visible Status

Labels such as Ready for Pickup, In Activity, Pending, and Verified are intended to make state easier to scan.

Confirmation

Success and optional-feature messages are intended to close the loop on important verification steps.

A Reusable System For The Proposal

Rather than treating every proposed page as a one-off, the concept repeats navigation, cards, form controls, actions, and status feedback. This gives different service areas a shared visual and interaction language.

Accessibility Considerations

The proposal considered readable hierarchy, clear labels, visible states, and controls that would not rely on colour alone. These decisions and settings would still require accessibility review and testing before implementation.

Outcome & Reflection

What The Proposal Demonstrates

The tender was not awarded, so the work remained a design proposal rather than a launched product. It demonstrates my ability to translate a broad service brief into mobile-first information architecture, connected user-flow concepts, and a reusable interface language.

If the proposal progressed, the next step would be to validate the pickup and payment flows with representative users, focusing on comprehension, error recovery, accessibility, and confidence at key confirmation points.

Clarity

Can users understand what is required at each stage?

Recovery

Can users easily recover from errors or incorrect selections?

Confidence

Do confirmation states make users feel certain that important actions were completed?
